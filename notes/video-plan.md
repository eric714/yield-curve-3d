# Video plan — yieldcurve3d.com

## The reframe that matters

Nobody searches YouTube for "yield curve 3D tutorial." They search for
**"what is an inverted yield curve"**, **"why did SVB fail"**, **"what is
quantitative easing."**

So don't make feature tours. Make **one video per idea**, and let the site be
how you show it. The features get taught anyway, as a byproduct, and the videos
are findable. A video called *"Steep, flat, inverted — and why anyone cares"*
will out-perform *"Using the height dropdown"* by an order of magnitude, and
teaches the same control.

Corollary: **each video is someone's first.** Assume no prior video watched.
Twenty seconds of orientation at the top, every time.

## Staying inside 5–7 minutes

The discipline is structural, not editorial. You cannot cut a long video down;
you have to build a short one.

1. **Write the one idea as a single sentence before you record.** If it needs
   an "and", it is two videos.
2. **Money shot by 90 seconds.** Not the finale — the hook. Show the striking
   thing, then explain it. Most people put it last and lose the audience first.
3. **No preamble.** No "hey everyone, welcome back". Open on the chart and a
   question: *"Why did borrowing money for two years cost more than for ten?"*
4. **Use the links.** Paste, load, record. Nothing kills pace like setting up
   controls on camera.
5. **Hide the panel.** Double-click empty space in the chart to collapse it —
   full-bleed chart for the visual sections, panel back when discussing it.
6. **Hard stop at seven.** Anything left over is the next video. That is a
   feature: you need topics anyway.

## Thumbnails and assets

The camera button in the corner exports a 2× image with the title, date range,
sources and watermark already on it. Screenshot the money shot, use it as the
thumbnail, and the site gets credited even when someone only sees the preview.

Choose light mode for thumbnails — it stands out in a feed of dark finance
charts.
