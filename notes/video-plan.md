## 1. Strategy

**Where these go.** A playlist called **The Shape of Money** on The Everyday
Trader. The audience already watches long market talks, so nothing here has to
recruit strangers. It has to be worth their time and send them to the site.

**Who is watching.** Retail traders. They know what the Fed is. They have heard
"inverted yield curve" and could not draw one.

**Format.** Five to seven minutes. Screen led, voice over the site. Face only
on the close, if at all. Dark mode for recording, light mode stills for
thumbnails, because they stand out in a feed of dark finance charts.

**The site is the product.** Each video teaches **one move** on
yieldcurve3d.com and sends people to **one deep link**. If a viewer finishes
and cannot name the move, the video failed regardless of how it performed.

**Layers start off, and go on in front of the viewer.** The site opens bare on
purpose. Every link below switches on only what that video discusses, and the
one toggle the video is about gets flipped on camera. The toggle is a beat.

### What I mean by "beat"

A **beat** is one movement: a single thing you say plus the single thing that
happens on screen while you say it. A six-minute video is roughly 10 to 14
beats. If a beat has no on-screen change, it is talking, and talking is what
makes videos run long.

Every beat below is **what you say** in bold quotes and *what you do* in
italics. Everything in quotes is meant to be said close to verbatim.

### Claims you may not inflate

This is the part that makes the playlist worth more than another explainer, so
it is a rule, not a preference. From `notes/methods.md`:

| You may say | You may not say |
|---|---|
| The curve shows what the bond market expects the Fed to do, and that guess has been well calibrated | That the curve beats economists, or that this is an edge |
| That finding is close to an identity: a 2-year yield largely **is** the market's guess at the next two years of Fed policy | That the chart forecasts the Fed |
| It does not predict the stock market | That an inversion is a sell signal, or a buy signal |
| A yield under CPI is a real loss. Name **CPI year over year** every time | "Inflation" unqualified. There are several |
| Inversion is a weak recession alarm, and 2022 to 2024 broke the folklore | That an inversion means a recession is coming |
| The Sahm rule is coincident, not early | That the Sahm rule predicts anything |
| QE video shows coincidence and scale, not cause | That QE lowered yields, on the evidence of this chart |
| Mode 2 is useless when the funds rate is parked, and does not show QE | That Mode 2 is the QE view |

**Never "so buy" or "so sell".** End on a use instead:

- **Mode 2 before an FOMC meeting**, to see what is already priced.
- **Mode 3 when someone says the curve is inverted**, to see how much and for
  how long.
- **The inflation sheet when CPI prints**, to see what lending actually earns.

### The recurring hook, once the five exist

Ninety seconds inside a regular Friday episode: **"the curve this week."** Open
the site live, move the window to the last month, read one number, drop the
link. No tutorial, no recap. It keeps the playlist alive between uploads and
costs nothing to produce.

## 2. Rollout

**Week 0. No video yet.** Inside a normal Everyday Trader episode, spend four
minutes on the live site. One picture, not a tour. Say a series is coming and
name it. Do not teach anything. The job is to make the playlist feel like it
came from the channel rather than arriving from nowhere.

**Weeks 1 to 5. One video per week, the same weekday.** Publish order is not
teaching order:

| Week | # | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **17** | The best picture on the site. Nothing to explain before it lands |
| 2 | **7** | The strongest claim, through one date |
| 3 | **1** | The definition. Pin it as "start here" the day it publishes |
| 4 | **2** | The idea people have already heard of |
| 5 | **19** | The audit. The answer to the question every viewer is holding |

**Then stop.** Look at watch time and link clicks before shooting anything
else. Only after that review do **12**, **11** and **20** get made.

**Do not publish all five in one day.** A playlist that appears fully formed
reads as a dump. One a week gives each one a chance and gives you five weeks of
comments to learn from.

**Do not treat videos 3 to 16 as a queue.** They are a catalog. Most of them
should never be shot.

## 3. Packaging

Same shape every episode so it becomes recognizable.

### Titles

The working titles below are for me. The channel titles are what someone taps.
Plain, specific, no adjectives, no question marks except where the video is
genuinely a question.

### Thumbnails

One site snapshot, **one date, one number.** No arrows, no circles, no face.
The camera button exports at 2x with the title, date range, sources and
watermark already on it, so the site gets credited even when someone only sees
the preview. Export light mode.

### The spoken close, word for word, every episode

About ten seconds. Same words every time, so regulars stop hearing it as an ad
and start hearing it as the outro.

> **"The chart is free. Nothing to install. I put this video's exact view in
> the description, same dates, same angle. Open it, drag across the surface,
> and you'll see the numbers I just read. yieldcurve3d.com"**

### Description template

Deep link on line 1, above the fold, before anything else.

```
Open this exact view: https://yieldcurve3d.com/[hash]
Free, runs in the browser. No account.
The Shape of Money, playlist: [playlist URL]
Not investment advice. Educational look at US Treasury data.
Sources: U.S. Treasury, FRED.
```

Each Tier 1 video below has this filled in with its own hash. `[playlist URL]`
is the one placeholder that stays until the playlist exists.

### Pinned comment

```
Exact view from this video: https://yieldcurve3d.com/[hash]
Whole series: [playlist URL]
```

### Mid-roll

**One poke, maximum**, and only at the moment a layer goes on:

> **"The link below opens with this layer already on."**

### End screen

Next video in the playlist, plus the website card.

### Shorts

One per episode. Twelve to fifteen seconds, no narration, URL on screen the
whole time. The Short is a picture with a caption, not a summary.

## 4. Tier 1, in publish order

Five videos, about half an hour of finished footage. **If you make only these,
the playlist works.** They deliver the best picture, make the strongest claim,
define the thing, give people the word they already half know, and then audit
the whole lot honestly. That last one is what separates this from every other
explainer, so it does not get left for later.

Two rules that came out of writing them:

**Load the link and look at the screen before writing the first beat.** Video 1
used to open end-on and then treat the end-on view as a reveal a minute later,
which gave it away in the first second. Video 7 used to open in Mode 2 and then
treat switching to Mode 2 as the money shot. Both fixed. Check yours.

**Put the day in the link and read the real numbers.** `&d=YYYY-MM-DD` opens
with the readout already up, so the figures are on screen before you say them.
Every number quoted below was read out of the shipped data. If one does not
match what you see, tell me rather than reading what is on screen. It means
something moved.

### Week 1 (catalog #17). The two years when lending lost money, 6 min

**One idea:** for 501 straight trading days, every Treasury paid less than
inflation.

`#from=2020-01-02&to=2023-12-29&m=level&w=none&v=default&t=dark&s=&d=2022-03-01`

*The link opens on the worst single day in the record with the readout up and
the inflation sheet **off**. Turning the sheet on is the money shot, so it must
not be on when you start.*

**0:00 to 0:25. Open cold on the number.**
- *No greeting. The readout is already showing 1 March 2022.*
- **"On this day, the United States government would pay you one point seven
  two per cent to lend it money for ten years. Prices were rising at eight
  point six. Read that as a single number and it is minus six point eight
  five per cent a year."**
- **"That is the worst day in thirty-six years of this data, and it happened
  in the asset everybody calls risk free."**

**0:25 to 1:10. The idea, slowly. This is the one people get wrong.**
- **"A yield is what you are paid. Inflation is what it is worth by the time
  you get it. The only number that matters is the difference."**
- Name the measure and keep naming it: **"Inflation here is CPI, year over
  year. There are other measures. This is that one."**

**1:10 to 2:10. MONEY SHOT: turn the sheet on.**
- *Open Show and tick "Inflation (CPI) as sea level". Let it settle. Say
  nothing for two seconds.*
- **"That sheet is CPI. It is drawn at the same height as the yields, on the
  same axis, so the gap is a distance instead of arithmetic."**
- **"Anything under it lost money. Not lost to a bad trade. Lost sitting
  still."**
- *This is the one mid-roll poke:* **"The link below opens with this layer
  already on."**

**2:10 to 3:20. Pull back and watch it go under.**
- *Press Play at 1x and let the window walk from early 2021 into mid 2022. Put
  your hands down.*
- **"Do not watch the yields going up. Watch the sheet going up faster."**
- **"1 March 2021 to 28 February 2023. Five hundred and one straight trading
  days with the entire curve, one month out to thirty years, underneath it.
  The longest run in the record."**

**3:20 to 4:00. The cleanest way to say it.**
- **"In the whole of 2022, two hundred and forty-nine trading days out of two
  hundred and forty-nine, there was not one single day when any maturity beat

**4:00 to 5:00. The turn.**
- *Press Play again through 2023.* The surface climbs back up through the
  sheet as the Fed hikes and inflation falls.
- *Type 2023-10-19 into Jump to and read "10yr after inflation" off the panel.*

**5:00 to 5:35. Context, so nobody thinks this was unique.**
- **"Negative real yields are not rare. The real ten-year has been negative on

- **"It was just never this deep, for this long, in living memory."**

**5:35 to 6:00. The use, then the close.**
- **"So here is when to open this. Next time CPI prints, put the sheet on and

- *Then the standard close, word for word. End on the surface.*

**Do not turn this into an argument for TIPS.** It is a video about reading a

### Week 2 (catalog #7). The bond market moves before the Fed does, 6 min

**One idea:** the market prices cuts months before the Fed makes them, and you
can see it as a shape.

`#from=2006-01-03&to=2009-12-31&m=level&w=none&v=default&t=dark&s=ff&d=2007-09-10`

*Opens in plain Yield mode with 10 September 2007 pinned and the Fed funds
target on the front edge. Switching to Mode 2 is the money shot, so do not
arrive already in it.*

**Title note:** this used to be called "forecasts the Fed". Two things killed
that word, and both are in the video. Do not put it back.

**0:00 to 0:20. Open cold on one date.**
- **"The tenth of September, 2007. The Fed is charging five and a quarter per
  cent. The two-year Treasury is paying three point eight seven. Eight days
  later the Fed cut."**
- **"The bond market had already moved. This is what that looks like."**

**0:20 to 1:15. Teach the mode before switching to it.**
- **"There is exactly one interest rate the Fed sets. Overnight. That is it.
  Every other number on this chart is set by people buying and selling."**
- *Point at the Fed funds staircase along the front edge.* **"That orange step
  is policy. Everything behind it is opinion."**
- **"So the useful question is never what a yield is. It is what a yield is
  compared to what the Fed is charging that same day."**

**1:15 to 2:15. MONEY SHOT: switch to Mode 2 on camera.**
- *Change Height of the surface from "Yield" to "Yield minus Fed funds". Do it
  slowly and let the surface resettle before talking.*
- **"Now the flat plane is the Fed itself. Zero means the market agrees with
  policy exactly."**
- **"Above the plane, the market wants paying more than the Fed pays. Below the
  plane, the market is betting the Fed is about to cut."**
- *Poke:* **"The link below opens in this mode already."**
- *The pin is still on 10 September 2007.* **"A point and a half below. That is
  not a hint. That is the whole bond market leaning on the door."**

**2:15 to 3:15. Scale, then the cut.**
- **"Across all of 2007, ninety-five per cent of every published point on this
  surface sat below the policy rate. Nineteen out of twenty."**
- *Jump to 2007-09-18.* **"Eight days later. Four and three-quarters."**

**3:15 to 4:15. Does it repeat? Three cycles.**
- *Put the three on screen together.* Trading days the 2-year crossed below the
  policy rate **and stayed there**, before the Fed's first cut:
  - Before the **January 2001** cut: **129 days** early
  - Before the **September 2007** cut: **307 days** early
  - Before the **September 2024** cut: **383 days** early
- **"Three easing cycles, three times the market got there first, by somewhere
  between six months and eighteen months."**
- **"And it is not always tidy. In the year before the 2001 cut the two-year
  was below policy on only a hundred and thirty-seven days out of two hundred
  and fifty-three. It crossed, uncrossed, and crossed again before it stuck."**

**4:15 to 5:15. The honesty beat. Do not skip it and do not rush it.**
- **"Now the part that stops this being a magic trick."**
- **"A two-year Treasury yield is roughly the average interest rate the market
  expects the Fed to set over the next two years. Subtract today's rate and
  what is left is the market's guess at how much the Fed will move."**
- **"So this chart is not predicting the Fed. It is showing you the market's
  guess, and the market's guess has been well calibrated. Those are different
  sentences, and only one of them is true."**
- **"It does not beat the market. It shows you the market. There is a whole
  video on how hard I tried to make that claim bigger and could not. It is the
  last one in this playlist."**

**5:15 to 5:40. When it is useless. Say it out loud.**
- *Press Play forward into 2009 and let the surface go flat against the plane.*
- **"From 2009 to 2014 this mode tells you nothing. The policy rate was a
  constant one eighth of a per cent, and subtracting a constant changes
  nothing. It is a tool for when policy is moving."**
- **"It is also not the QE view, whatever your instinct says. Same reason."**

**5:40 to 6:00. The use, then the close.**
- **"So here is when to open this. The week before an FOMC meeting, switch to
  Mode 2 and look at where the surface sits. That is what is already priced.
  Whatever the Fed does that is not already in that picture is the part that
  moves markets."**
- *Standard close.*

**Packaging**
- **Channel title:** The bond market knew. The Fed hadn't moved yet.
- **Thumbnail:** Mode 2, September 2007, surface visibly below the plane. One
  date, `10 SEP 2007`. One number, `-1.38`.
- **Short (14s):** the Mode 1 to Mode 2 switch, then a slow pan as the gap

### Week 3 (catalog #1). What a yield curve actually is, 6 min

**One idea:** the government borrows for different lengths of time and pays a
different rate for each; that set of rates on one day is the curve.

`#from=2021-08-25&to=2026-08-25&m=level&w=none&v=default&t=dark&s=&d=2026-08-03`

it makes it worse at that job. Pin it as "start here" the day it publishes.

- No intro card, no greeting, no name. The first thing on screen is the thing.

- **"When the government needs money, it sells you a bond. You hand over cash,
  it pays you interest, and years later it gives your money back."**
- **"It does that for a lot of different lengths at the same time. One month.
  Two years. Thirty years. Each one has its own interest rate."**
- *Point along the near edge as you name the lengths.*

**1:30 to 2:30. Read a real day. Three numbers, not four.**

**2:30 to 4:30. Back to three dimensions.**

**4:30 to 6:00. Close on the question.**

- **"That is what the rest of these are about."**
- **"So here is the one thing to do with it today. Open it, type today's date,

### Week 4 (catalog #2). Steep, flat, inverted, 6 min

**One idea:** the shape carries information the level does not.

`#from=2021-09-01&to=2023-12-29&m=level&w=none&v=front&t=dark&s=`

*Opens end-on, which is correct here: this video is about shape, and the reveal
at the end is Mode 3, not the camera angle.*

**Do not say an inversion means a recession is coming.** The folklore beat is
in this video, and it is a debunk. The alarm itself gets its own video later.

**0:00 to 0:25. Open cold.**
- **"Normally it costs more to borrow money for ten years than for two. That
  is the whole idea of lending. Sometimes it costs less. That is what you are
  looking at, and it is worth knowing why."**
- *Let the shot sit for three seconds before continuing.*

**0:25 to 1:00. One sentence of recap, then move.**
- **"Fourteen maturities across, one month on the left, thirty years on the
  right. Every trading day is one line going back."**
- **"Everything I am about to say is about the tilt of that shape. Not how
  high it is. The tilt."**

**1:00 to 2:00. The two shapes that are not news.**
- *Click the "Cuts and steepening" preset.* **"Steep. Long money costs more.
  This is the textbook picture and most of history looks like it."**
- *Click the "Greenspan's conundrum" preset.* **"Flat. The market has stopped
  expecting much in either direction."**

**2:00 to 3:00. MONEY SHOT: the tip-over.**
- *Click "Inflation shock", which is exactly the opening range, and put the
  camera back to front-on.*
- **"Now watch."** *Press Play and let it run from late 2021 into 2023.* The
  surface tips over. The left side climbs above the right.
- **"Inverted. Two-year money now costs more than ten-year money. The market
  is saying it expects rates to come down, which means it expects something to
  go wrong."**

**3:00 to 3:40. Put a number on it.**
- *Type 2023-07-03 into Jump to.*
- **"Two-year, four point nine four. Ten-year, three point eight six. A gap of
  minus one point zero eight, and that is the deepest that gap gets anywhere in
  thirty-six years."**

**3:40 to 4:30. The folklore, and what the record actually says.**
- **"You have heard that an inversion predicts a recession. Here is what this
  data says about that, and it is more interesting than the folklore."**
- *Four numbers on screen, left up:* inverted days in the two years before each
  recession. **227 before 2001. 238 before 2008.** Then: **16 before 1990, and
  the record starts in January 1990 so it misses most of that one. 3 before
  2020.**
- **"And then 2022. The longest inversion in the record, five hundred and
  thirty-seven trading days, and no recession followed at all."**
- **"Five hundred and sixty-eight of the one thousand and fifty-two inverted
  days in this record had no recession start within two years. More than
  half."**
- **"It is a warning light, not a prophecy. And it is a warning light with a
  false alarm still fresh in the memory."**

### Staying inside 5 to 7 minutes
1. **Write the one idea as a single sentence first.** If it needs an "and", it
   is two videos.
2. **Money shot by 90 seconds.** It is the hook, not the finale.
3. **No greeting.** Open on the chart and a sentence that makes someone want
   the answer.
4. **Every beat needs an on-screen change.** If nothing moves, cut it.
5. **Hard stop at seven.** Leftovers are the next video, and you need topics.

### Recording
- **Paste the link, then record.** Never set up controls on camera, except the
  one toggle the video is about, which *should* be on camera.
- **Pasting into a tab that already has the site open works.** Some of these
  links differ only after the "#", and a browser will not reload for that. It
  is handled. You can run a whole session in one tab.
- **A link without a maturity list means all fourteen.** Video 12 trims the
  axis; the next link no longer inherits the trim.
- **A link can open on a pinned day.** `&d=YYYY-MM-DD` puts the readout up
  before you speak. Use it for the first pin in a video and the Jump to field
  for later ones. A date outside the link's range is ignored, so keep them
  consistent.
- **Where a beat says Play, press Play.** It walks the trading days on its own
  so you can put your hands down and talk. Three speeds: turtle at about four
  trading days a second, 1x at twelve, rabbit at forty. Pick the one that makes
  the move take about as long as the sentence describing it.

  So you can let it run, interrupt to make a point, and carry on without the
  chart moving under you.
- **Where a beat says jump to a date, type it into Jump to.** Hunting for one
  day by dragging looks like fumbling on camera. It snaps to the nearest
  trading day and pins it, and slides the window if the day is outside it.
- **Double-click empty chart space** to hide the side panel for full-bleed
  shots. The ☰ button brings it back.
- **The readout draws the day as a curve** above the numbers, so a video about
  the shape of one day does not need the end-on view.
- **The readout always shows CPI and the real ten-year yield**, whatever else
  is on. You can read the inflation-adjusted number on any day in any video

- **The `?` button replays the walkthrough**, eleven steps. A decent free

## Appendix B: what the three height modes mean

The chart draws a surface, and the **height** of that surface is a number you
choose. Three choices, three different questions.

**Mode 1: Yield.** The rate itself. What the government paid to borrow, that
day, for that length of time. Where you should spend most of your time.

**Mode 2: Yield minus Fed funds.** Every point measured against the rate the
Fed was charging that same day. The flat zero plane **is** the Fed.

The Fed sets exactly one rate, overnight. Every other rate on the chart is set
by the market, so this mode isolates the gap between the two. A surface below
zero means the market is betting on cuts.

The clean example: on **10 September 2007** the 2-year sat **1.38 points below**
what the Fed was charging, and the Fed cut eight days later. Across 2007,
**95 per cent** of the published curve sat below the policy rate.

Across the last three easing cycles, the 2-year crossed below the policy rate
and **stayed** there this far ahead of the Fed's first cut:

| First cut | Days the market was there first |
|---|---|
| January 2001 | 129 trading days |
| September 2007 | 307 trading days |
| September 2024 | 383 trading days |

**When Mode 2 is useless:** when the Fed is parked. From 2009 to 2014 the
policy rate was a constant 0.125 per cent, so subtracting it changes nothing.
That stretch spans 4.85 points in both modes, the same number to two decimals.
**It is not the QE view.** It genuinely does not show QE, and the instinct that
it should is the single most common wrong idea about this chart.

**Mode 3: Yield minus 3-month.** Every point measured against that same day's
3-month bill. This is the slope, and anything below the zero plane is an
inversion.

The difference from Mode 2 is the baseline, and it matters:

- **Mode 2's baseline is a decision.** The Fed chose that number in a meeting.
- **Mode 3's baseline is a price.** The market set it.

So Mode 2 asks *"what does the market think of the Fed?"* and Mode 3 asks
*"what shape is the curve?"* Worth saying out loud in any video that switches.

## Appendix C: the inflation sheet

Inflation is a translucent sheet floating **inside** the chart at the height of
the CPI inflation rate, under **Show**. Better than a back wall for one reason:
**it shares the vertical axis with the surface.** A wall sits beside the data
and you compare by eye. The sheet cuts through it, so the gap you care about is
a distance you can see.

**Anything under the sheet lost money in real terms.** A 3 per cent yield when
prices rise 5 per cent is a 2 per cent annual loss dressed up as income.

- In the whole of **2022**, on **249 of 249 trading days**, no Treasury out to
  thirty years paid more than inflation.
- The longest unbroken run with the entire curve below CPI is **501 trading
  days, 1 March 2021 to 28 February 2023.**
- The worst single day was **1 March 2022**: the ten-year paid **1.72 per
  cent** against CPI at **8.6**, a real yield of **−6.85 per cent.**
- Across the record the real ten-year yield was negative on **1,557 of 9,171
  days, 17 per cent of the time.**

**Always name the measure.** There are several inflation numbers, and the site
uses **CPI, year over year.** Say "CPI" out loud, every time.
